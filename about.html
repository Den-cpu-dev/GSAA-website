Our Story

GSAA Overview

The Ghana Sociological and Anthropological Association (GSAA) was re-launched in 2013 and expanded in 2014 to include anthropology and social work in a shared national social science platform.

Mission

Platform for Social Scientists

"To advance the scientific study of society and social problems, while providing policy-relevant social science knowledge."

GSAA provides a national platform for mobilizing and connecting sociologists, anthropologists, social workers, and related social scientists across Ghana for scholarship, policy engagement, and professional exchange.

The association also promotes teaching and learning in higher education, encourages African and global collaboration, and works to strengthen the visibility and relevance of sociological, anthropological, and social work analysis in Ghanaian society.

Aims

Association Priorities

Mobilize & Connect
Bring together sociologists, anthropologists, social workers, and related social scientists in Ghana.
Exchange & Networking
Serve as a forum for critical dialogue, idea exchange, and professional networking among members.
Teaching & Learning
Promote sociology, anthropology, and social work teaching in public and private higher education institutions.
Journal Development
Strengthen the Ghana Journal of Sociology and Anthropology as a mouthpiece for quality peer-reviewed scholarship.
History

Re-Launch and Growth

2013
Re-Launch of the Association
On 1 March 2013, the Ghana Sociological Association was re-launched at Yiri Lodge, University of Ghana, under the theme "Sociology and National Development," after over two decades of dormancy.
2014
Expansion to GSAA
A decision was taken to broaden the association beyond sociologists to include anthropologists and social workers, and the body was renamed the Ghana Sociological and Anthropological Association (GSAA).
2015
First GSAA Conference
The first conference was held in November 2015 at Yiri Lodge, University of Ghana, on "Social Science Knowledge Production in Africa: Interrogating the Past for Future Directions and Actions."
2017
Second Conference at KNUST
Held on 21-22 September 2017 at the Innovative Learning Centre, KNUST, under "The Social Scientist and Sustainable Development," with 62 participants from UG, KNUST, UCC, UDS, Mountcrest, and Dominion University.
2018-2024
Rotational Annual Meetings
GSAA meetings have continued in September each year on a rotational basis hosted by Sociology and Anthropology departments in UG, KNUST, and UCC.
2025
9th GSAA Conference
The latest (9th) GSAA conference was hosted by the Sociology Department, University of Ghana, in August 2025, continuing broad disciplinary engagement across sociology, anthropology, and social work.
Current
Conference and Policy Engagement
Conference papers and GSAA deliberations continue to address social issues of national importance and strengthen evidence-based social science engagement in Ghana.
Leadership

Association Structure

Until the association is firmly established, the National Secretariat is lodged at the Sociology Department, University of Ghana, Legon. The Management Committee includes institutional representatives and works with the National Coordinator to run day-to-day affairs.

WB
National Coordinator
Prof. William Boateng

The current National Coordinator, Professor William Boateng (University of Cape Coast), works with the Secretariat to administer and coordinate GSAA affairs and Management Committee operations.

Contact Secretariat
AS
National Secretariat
Sociology Department, University of Ghana

The secretariat serves as the administrative base of GSAA and supports communication, records, and membership coordination under departmental oversight.

SC
Management Committee
Institutional Representatives

Each member institution has representation on the Management Committee, which meets regularly to deliberate on GSAA affairs and social issues of national importance.

KA
Anchor Institutions
UG, KNUST, UCC, UDS

Teaching staff in anchor universities are mandated to be members and provide a core institutional base for GSAA governance and activities.

EO
Wider Membership Base
Public & Private Universities

Other institutions include UBIDS, UNIMAC, University of Education, and Central University, with ongoing plans for wider outreach and membership drive.

YD
Annual Meetings
Rotational Hosting Model

Annual meetings are hosted rotationally by member departments, reinforcing institutional collaboration across Ghanaian universities.

Governance

Constitution &
Governance

The affairs of GSAA are currently guided by an Interim Constitution, which remains in force until a permanent constitution is ratified at an annual meeting of the Association.

This framework guides governance, administration, and membership accountability while the association continues institutional consolidation and growth.
