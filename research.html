Areas of Research

Six interconnected research clusters driving sociological and anthropological inquiry across Ghana and the broader African continent.

Research Clusters

Understanding Ghanaian Society

GSAA members contribute to six core research domains that reflect the most pressing questions in Ghanaian and African social science. Each cluster brings together scholars, postgraduates, and practitioners working on shared themes.

01
Chieftaincy & Traditional Governance
Examining the evolving role of traditional authority in contemporary Ghanaian governance, law, and community life. Research explores how traditional rulers navigate constitutional roles, mediate land disputes, and serve as custodians of cultural identity across Ghana's 16 regions.
Land tenure Customary law Palace politics Cultural preservation Succession disputes
02
Kinship, Family & Social Structure
Exploring matrilineal and patrilineal systems, gender roles, and the transformation of family in urbanising Ghana. Studies examine how lineage systems shape inheritance, childcare, and marriage - and how urbanisation is reshaping these age-old structures.
Matriliny Marriage practices Gender dynamics Childcare systems Inheritance
03
Religion, Belief & Social Life
Investigating how Christianity, Islam, and indigenous spiritual practices shape identity and community cohesion. Scholars examine how pluralistic religious landscapes co-exist and compete in shaping public morality and political participation in Ghana.
Pentecostalism Islamic communities Indigenous belief Religious pluralism Faith & politics
04
Urbanisation & Migration
Understanding the social consequences of rapid urban growth, internal migration, and diaspora formations. Research tracks rural-urban migration patterns, informal settlements, and the social capital networks migrants build in Ghanaian cities.
Rural-urban migration Informal economy Diaspora Slum studies Social networks
05
Development & Social Policy
Bridging social science research with policy to address poverty, inequality, health, and education in Ghana. Work bridges ethnography with policy analysis to assess the social impact of national programmes including LEAP, NHIS, and Free SHS.
Social protection Health systems Education policy Poverty analysis Programme evaluation
06
Identity, Ethnicity & Nationhood
Tracing how ethnic identities, pan-Africanism, and national consciousness shape social relations across Ghana. Studies interrogate how ethnic identities are mobilised in politics, how diaspora communities negotiate belonging, and pan-African solidarity today.
Ethnic politics Pan-Africanism National identity Diaspora belonging Cultural citizenship

Methodology

Research Approaches

GSAA scholars employ a wide range of methodologies rooted in both global social science traditions and African epistemological frameworks.

Ethnography & Fieldwork
Long-term participant observation, community immersion, and thick description remain central to our anthropological tradition. GSAA members conduct fieldwork in every region of Ghana.
Survey & Quantitative Analysis
Large- and small-scale surveys, statistical modelling, and demographic analysis are used to identify patterns and test hypotheses across populations.
Mixed Methods
Combining qualitative depth with quantitative breadth, many GSAA projects use sequential or concurrent mixed-methods designs to address complex social questions.
Oral History & Narrative
Collecting and interpreting oral traditions, life histories, and narrative accounts - particularly valuable for researching communities with rich oral traditions.
Participatory Action Research
Community-engaged research where participants co-design studies and apply findings. Increasingly used in policy-oriented work on health, education, and development.
Digital & Computational Methods
Emerging approaches including digital ethnography, social media analysis, GIS mapping, and computational text analysis of archival and media sources.
